## Why it exists

A patient reads a lab result in their portal and does not understand it: *"My A1c came back 7.8 — what does that mean?"* An assistant that explains it is genuinely useful. An assistant that answers **everything** is a liability, because the same box will also receive *"should I stop taking my metformin?"*, *"do I have lupus?"* and *"I have crushing chest pain."*

Most healthcare AI demos put a disclaimer in the system prompt and call it safety. That is hard to test, easy to bypass and impossible to measure. A healthcare organization needs the opposite: safety rules that sit outside the model, have their own tests and publish their own numbers — so that a clinical owner, not the model vendor, decides how cautious the assistant should be.

## How it works

Policy runs **before** the model and again **after** it — a guardrail sandwich. The second half is not a replay of the first; it judges the retrieved evidence and the drafted answer on its own authority.

- **Pre-flight screen:** emergencies and decisions only a clinician can make are stopped before the AI reads anything.
- **Read the record:** the model looks up labs, medications and conditions for the selected patient only.
- **Post-flight check:** the draft is split into claims; each clinical claim must resolve to something actually retrieved, and critical values override the draft.
- **Publish, hold or replace:** code — not the AI — publishes the answer, holds it for a clinician, or swaps in a safe pre-written reply.

### Why two independent halves

Take *"What does my potassium of 6.9 mean?"* It is a benign education question, so the pre-flight screen correctly allows it. But the retrieved value is above the critical-high threshold — a medical emergency — and only the post-flight half can know that. It overrides the draft with an urgent safety message and routes the case to clinician review. A single up-front filter cannot do this.

## Results and honest limits

- **Gating every pull request:** a canonical evaluation of 16 cases (18 runs) that needs no model key, plus lint, strict typing, import contracts and tests.
- **Live-model check:** the same 16 cases passed 16 of 16 against the real model on the last run.

<Callout type="limit">
The live run is not recorded or gated, so it is a smoke test rather than a measured rate. A larger stratified evaluation with hand-labelled cases and confidence intervals — and a statistically meaningful latency and cost sample — has not been done yet.
</Callout>

## Engineering decisions

- **A refusal never reaches a patient tool.** This is enforced by the shape of the graph, not by a check inside a node.
- **Substring matching is not a guardrail.** A metaphor can defeat a keyword filter and an attributed mention must not trigger one, so classification is explicit and tested against both failure modes.
- **Publication is deterministic.** Final text is chosen by code from approved outcomes after the checks pass.
- **Citations are real.** Education links point to public-domain National Library of Medicine sources, and lookups use exact matching before semantic search.
- **Every answer shows its work.** Held answers wait in a durable queue and resume when a clinician decides, and the interface streams a step-by-step timeline with a per-node model and cost meter.

## Who it's for

For **health systems and digital-health teams**, it is a pattern for patient-facing AI where the clinical owner controls the boundaries. For **engineering leaders**, it shows how to turn "the model should be careful" into components you can test and measure.
